# Flipside — Editorial Mission

## Purpose

Flipside exists to make media bias visible without taking sides itself.

The goal is not to tell readers what to think — it's to show them that two people can read entirely different accounts of the same event, both from "reputable" sources, and walk away with opposite impressions of reality. That gap is the story.

We do not editorialize. We curate. The pairing is the argument.

---

## Editorial Principles

1. **Pair, don't preach.** Every story on Flipside has a left source and a right source. No orphaned stories.
2. **Reputable outlets only.** No fringe sites, no blogs, no substacks. Outlets with editorial standards and track records.
3. **Same story, different frame.** The best pairings are when both sides are technically accurate but create completely different impressions.
4. **Headlines should crackle.** Short, punchy, Drudge-style. Not neutral wire copy.
5. **Photos for top stories.** The lead story on each side gets an illustrative image. Photos should be evocative, not literal.
6. **Human has final say.** AI suggests. Editor decides. Nothing goes live without approval.

## Story Categories

Stories can be tagged with one or more of the following:
- `politics` — Elections, legislation, government
- `economy` — Markets, jobs, inflation, trade
- `foreign` — International affairs, war, diplomacy
- `culture` — Social issues, education, religion, identity
- `crime` — Law enforcement, courts, justice
- `media` — Press, censorship, platform policy
- `science` — Climate, health, technology

---

## What Makes a Good Flipside Story

The best stories are ones where:
- Both sides are covering it, but the framing diverges sharply
- The headline choice alone tells you which side wrote it
- A reader of only one side would have a genuinely different understanding of events
- There's a strong photo opportunity (for top story)

Avoid: pure opinion pieces, stories only one side is covering, celebrity gossip, local news without national resonance.
